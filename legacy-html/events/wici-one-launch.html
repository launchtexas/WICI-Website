Overview

Join us in New York for the launch of WiCi One, a wireless, local AI GPU.

Today, AI runs in one of two places. In the cloud, which is powerful but far away and paid for monthly. Or on your device, which is private but only as capable as the phone or laptop in your hand.

WiCi One is a new option. It's a GPU that sits in your home or office and connects to your devices over Wi-Fi. Your phone, laptop and desktop get GPU power without a cable and without a graphics card of their own. Your data stays with you.

Every demo runs on an ordinary phone, laptop or desktop, with a WiCi One nearby doing the heavy lifting, using WiCi's proprietary wireless computing technology. Demos are guided by the WiCi team and take 30 minutes.

What You'll Experience

See a wireless GPU power lightweight, fanless devices through real work. Every device on the floor is standard. The GPU is across the room.

  • Local AI

    Voice assistants, language models and image generation, all running on site. No cloud.

  • Gaming

    PC games rendered across the room and played on a thin, fanless laptop.

  • Creative Work

    3D rendering on regular desktops, several at once, sharing one GPU.

  • Hands-on with the hardware. See the device opened up at the tech table. Full specifications are announced at the event.
  • Time with the founder. Dr. Zili Meng, WiCi's co-founder and CEO and professor of electronic and computer engineering, is on site both days.

Why It Matters

Today, a GPU can only power the device it's built into. When you need more, you buy new hardware, switch to another machine or send your work to the cloud.
Each comes with a cost:

  • Cost. More devices means more GPUs, or another subscription. Most of that hardware sits unused. Extended reading: Tokenomics: Why making AI pay is tricky, BBC
  • Latency. Sending work to the cloud adds delay. Fine for a chatbot. Not for a robot. Extended reading: A Brain Too Big to Carry: On-Device vs Datacenter Inference, SemiAnalysis
  • Mobility. Demanding creative work stays at the desk. Laptops with GPUs run hot and heavy. Extended reading: Razer Blade 16 (2026) review, Tom's Hardware
  • Privacy. Cloud processing sends your conversations and documents off site. Extended reading: Could Your Chatbot Be Stealing Ideas From You?, The New York Times
