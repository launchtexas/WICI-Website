Solutions · Virtual reality

Move the compute.
Lighten the headset.

A virtual reality headset carries displays, optics, sensors, compute, cooling, and the battery to power them. WiCi moves GPU-heavy spatial computing to a nearby device, giving headset designers room to build smaller, lighter eyewear.

  1. In the headset Everything rides on your face. Compute, cooling, and battery capacity compete for space inside a head-worn enclosure.
  2. In the cloud Powerful, but too far away. Every frame depends on the internet path, adding variable delay and sending the room outside the premises.
  3. Nearby Heavy compute, off your face. A full-size GPU on the premises, reached over one local wireless hop.

The WiCi approach

Nearby spatial compute
for lightweight VR.

A full-size GPU in the room, one wireless hop from the headset, over a link built for interactive workloads.

A headset needs fast local sensing and display control. A desktop GPU provides the rendering and AI capacity that wearables cannot comfortably carry. WiCi splits the work at that boundary: tracking and late-stage reprojection stay on the headset, while heavy spatial workloads run nearby.

WiCi One is the on-premises GPU. The Overview presents it over standard Wi‑Fi with a measured ~1.5 ms base round trip. The result is a local path between the glasses and the GPU, with no datacenter hop and no internet dependency.

A deliberate split between headset and GPU.

Comfort improves only when the system moves the right work. The WiCi architecture separates the hard real-time display loop from workloads that can run nearby.

  • Keep pose tracking local. The headset continues sensing motion and controlling the final display timing.
  • Offload the heavy work. Rendering, reconstruction, scene models, and AI run on WiCi One.
  • Schedule the wireless path. Interactive traffic needs predictable access to the nearby GPU.
  • Degrade gracefully. The headset keeps a safe local experience if the nearby link is interrupted.
